abstract: Glucocorticoids (GCs) are potent repressors of NF-κB activity, making them a preferred choice
  for treatment of inflammation-driven conditions. Despite the widespread use of GCs in the clinic, current
  models are inadequate to explain the role of the glucocorticoid receptor (GR) within this critical signaling
  pathway. GR binding directly to NF-κB itself-tethering in a DNA binding-independent manner-represents
  the standing model of how GCs inhibit NF-κB-driven transcription. We demonstrate that direct binding
  of GR to genomic NF-κB response elements (κBREs) mediates GR-driven repression of inflammatory gene
  expression. We report five crystal structures and solution NMR data of GR DBD-κBRE complexes, which
  reveal that GR recognizes a cryptic response element between the binding footprints of NF-κB subunits
  within κBREs. These cryptic sequences exhibit high sequence and functional conservation, suggesting
  that GR binding to κBREs is an evolutionarily conserved mechanism of controlling the inflammatory response.
