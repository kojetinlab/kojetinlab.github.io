abstract: Protein arginine phosphorylation is a recently discovered modification that affects multiple
  cellular pathways in Gram-positive bacteria. In particular, the phosphorylation of arginine residues
  by McsB is critical for regulating the cellular stress response. Given that the highly efficient protein
  arginine phosphatase YwlE prevents arginine phosphorylation under non-stress conditions, we hypothesized
  that this enzyme negatively regulates arginine phosphorylation and acts as a sensor of cell stress.
  To evaluate this hypothesis, we developed the first suite of highly potent and specific SO3-amidine-based
  YwlE inhibitors. With these protein arginine phosphatase-specific probes, we demonstrated that YwlE
  activity is suppressed by oxidative stress, which consequently increases arginine phosphorylation, thereby
  inducing the expression of stress-response genes, which is critical for bacterial virulence. Overall,
  we predict that these novel chemical tools will be widely used to study the regulation of protein arginine
  phosphorylation in multiple organisms.
