abstract: Peroxisome proliferator-activated receptors (PPARs) are pharmacological targets for the treatment
  of metabolic disorders. Previously, we demonstrated the anti-diabetic effects of SR1664, a PPARγ modulator
  lacking classical transcriptional agonism, despite its poor pharmacokinetic properties. Here, we report
  identification of the antagonist SR11023 as a potent insulin sensitizer with significant plasma exposure
  following oral administration. To determine the structural mechanism of ligand-dependent antagonism
  of PPARγ, we employed an integrated approach combining solution-phase biophysical techniques to monitor
  activation helix (helix 12) conformational dynamics. While informative on receptor dynamics, hydrogen/deuterium
  exchange mass spectrometry and nuclear magnetic resonance data provide limited information regarding
  the specific orientations of structural elements. In contrast, label-free quantitative crosslinking
  mass spectrometry revealed that binding of SR11023 to PPARγ enhances interaction with co-repressor motifs
  by pushing H12 away from the agonist active conformation toward the H2-H3 loop region (i.e., the omega
  loop), revealing the molecular mechanism for active antagonism of PPARγ.
